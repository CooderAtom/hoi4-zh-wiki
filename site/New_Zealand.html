The 新西兰自治领 is a  英国 自治领 in Oceania with a core population of 1.49 M and an additional 113.35 K non-core. Its closest neighbor is  澳大利亚 to the west.

历史背景

New Zealand declared war on Germany on 3 September 1939, following the United Kingdom's declaration. Unlike Australia, New Zealand made its own formal declaration.

Despite its small population of around 1.6 million at the time, New Zealand made a significant contribution to the war. Over 200,000 New Zealanders served in the armed forces, with the Royal New Zealand Air Force (RNZAF) playing a major role in the European and Pacific theaters. Many New Zealand airmen served with the Royal Air Force, while others were involved in maritime patrols and escort duties.

The Royal New Zealand Navy (RNZN) participated in naval operations from the beginning of the war, including escorting convoys and engaging enemy vessels. New Zealand’s Army was involved in key campaigns in North Africa, Greece, and Italy, with the 2nd New Zealand Division distinguishing itself in battles such as El Alamein and Monte Cassino.

After Japan entered the war in late 1941, New Zealand shifted its focus to the Pacific, where it worked closely with the United States and Australia to combat the Japanese advance. While New Zealand itself was not directly attacked, its forces were engaged in major battles in the Solomon Islands and other Pacific campaigns.

主条目：New Zealand national focus tree


New Zealand's unique focus tree has five main branches:

The First Labour Government
Acts as the political tree, providing both a means to deal with internal issues, and mutually exclusive branches on whether to remain within the Commonwealth, or to push for Independence as a Democratic, Fascist or Communist country.
Transfer the New Zealand Division
Focuses on developing a navy through construction of dockyards and bonuses to ship and naval doctrine research.
Expand the NZPAF
Provides bonuses to aircraft and air doctrine research.
Bureau of Industry/Ministry of Public Works
Acts as the industrial tree, buffing industrial capacity, resource extraction and research slots.
Army Reforms
Focuses on bonuses for Infantry, Armour and Artillery, and construction of military industry capacity.

地理

太平洋中的新西兰

New Zealand is located in the Pacific Ocean, just east of Australia and south of France's New Caledonia and the United Kingdom's Fiji islands. New Zealand consists of two moderately-sized island 州 connected by a single strait. New Zealand owns Samoa, an island chain composed of two provinces, both of which are plains, located northeast of Fiji.

The North Island consists mostly of forests. There are some hills in the northeast and plains dotting the island, as well as some mountainous provinces: one in the center and one in the uppermost northeast near the hills. This state is the location of Wellington, New Zealand's 10 victory point capital, which is the location of a level two naval base. In the northeast is a level three air base, which is level eight at the start of the 1939 scenario. To the northwest is Auckland, a five victory point city that houses a level three naval base.

The South Island is mostly mountainous, but contains some hills, plains, and a single forest in the south and southeast, as well as one additional forest in the north. Like North Island, it has an air and naval base. Both are level one and located on the east side of the island and on single victory point provinces — the air base in Dunedin in the south and the naval base in Christchurch in the north.

策略与指南

以下是one of many玩家为新西兰建议的战略。请记住，由于游戏的动态特性，其他玩家的情况可能会有所不同。

1936 Scenario without independence

As New Zealand your biggest starting limitations are manpower and research slots. However NZ has a pretty good focus tree so that will help out a lot. A really through guide is below so this is just an alternative to that. NZ has good tank focuses quite early which means you can get up to the 1936 armour quite quickly.

As you are limited on manpower focus on building a few really good armour divisions. Then micro them in North Africa quickly pushing the Italians out, again in East Africa. Then move your army to Malaya, and once war starts with Japan push north overrunning Japanese and Siam infantry. Bangkok will fall in short order.

For the airforce and navy focus on submarines and naval bombers. These will do extraordinary damage to the Italian, then to the Japanese fleet. Don't bother building anything else.

In the national focus tree after the tank bonuses focus on the industrial ones to get more resources and factories and tech slots and separately the Maori ones as these will give you extra manpower.

1936 Scenario: Independence, but with the Allies and in Multiplayer

Setup and national focus progression:

Immediately release 萨摩亚 as a puppet state. They will serve you far better as a puppet than as true territory, as in this way they can get all the free goodies that the generic focus tree provides, while still performing their role as an air and naval base in the pacific. They will be lucky to field even a single division, but that can still provide a garrison that you do not have to spend valuable manpower on. It will also result in, when you do eventually annex them, 3 military factories, 4 civilian factories, and 3 Naval Dockyards, in addition to whatever handful of destroyers and military equipment they may have built. Releasing Samoa will save you about 4.03K of manpower - since, when it gets released as 傀儡国, garrison gets sent back home.

Point is, they are essentially savings bonds, with no drawback whatsoever. You cannot deploy to Samoa or any other place besides New Zealand, and they serve the exact same purpose regardless. As such, when you acquire the pacific holdings of the US, France, UK, and (if you are early enough) Japan, Immediately release them as puppet states, as they serve the same purpose as Samoa, besides possibly Hawaii, who may provide a division or two towards the cause, as well as most likely a token naval force.

You should choose for your starting focus "Transfer the New Zealand division" and continue down the naval path until you have dockyards. Then set your 2 cruisers to do exercises, and the dockyards can repair them. Once that is done, rush down the "Maori affairs"(?) side of the political tree, taking the Maori volunteers route at the split at the bottom, and skipping the original Maori Affairs Act for now. Then rush down the political side that has you go independent, but do not take the final focus yet, or you will leave the allies, and be left on your own against Japan. Then complete the naval tree, choosing submarines over destroyers, and skipping light cruiser effort.

Then you must start the industrial branch, and this is quite late, but it does work. Rush down each tree in a bid to get all four research slots open ASAP. By this time it is 1942 or so, and you have the best research capabilities in the world, excepting maybe the US (and Germany if they go democratic). With commonwealth research and the fact that the commonwealth is already fairly advanced into 1936, you should be able to focus on naval and air technologies, allowing you to field the most technologically advanced navy in the game.

This is to the point where you can have 1 or even 2 slots dedicated to ahead of time research, while the other four benefit from commonwealth research, and you soon have a terrifying little devil on your hands. Ignore your army past 1939, they should only be as a last resort if the Japanese ever get to you.

From here, you have a variety of options you could take in the course of the war. In the game I pulled this in, midway through the war, the Australian player and I left the allies and formed a nice little haven in the pacific. We were roleplaying a bit of course, so we stayed as this, while supporting the invasion of Japan. One last tip: always be building a dockyard, as they do not count towards required civilian factories, and thus you can get out around 3.5 a year with your fully focused civilian factories and the ones from your puppets.

Army:

The new Zealander army is quite tiny, and should only be invested in enough to get standard 7 infantry/ 2 artillery divisions, with 6 of these in order to garrison your two airbases, all of your victory points, and all naval bases. If a naval invasion threatens, move to defend against it, but on the whole, plan on your Maori Volunteers being your main expeditionary force, along with maybe a single tank division, and possibly a division of marines.

Invest minimally into army research, only up to 1939, and let the Australians and Americans handle the land side of the pacific war. Your job is going to be to take out the Japanese navy.

Navy:

New Zealand's navy is nonexistent at the start, but within a few years can become quite formidable. You should start with a few escort destroyers to protect shipping with the US, and then a group of scout destroyers to support your patrol cruisers. You should also build 2 more classes of destroyer, a mainline medium destroyer that you can mass produce as your primary screens, as well as a heavy destroyer, almost a light cruiser, to participate as a primary combatant.

For your Submarine force (and you should have one), first produce a few heavy submarines armed to the teeth. These can provide a raiding fleet that is far more effective than the same amount of time of light subs. You should have a group of tiny submarines with mine laying capability, and later on, a larger group of light submarines. After this, have your dockyards ready to produce a heavy carrier, which can field 50 aircraft and with secondary guns for defenses.

Make sure you have naval aircraft researched. This should take about a year to build with 5 dockyards (which you should have 9 of, by this point). Straight after, you should build a light battlecruiser, to have a ship capable of supporting your destroyers and your cruisers. Continue building like this, and as soon as the battle cruiser is done, it should be late 1943.

By now, with your superior research you should have 1944 carrier hulls. Design a heavy carrier with room for 100 aircraft (by the way you should be using the pacific fleet designer that grants +25% deck space), and assign your dockyards to build 2 simultaneously. With your remaining ones continue to construct destroyers and your light submarines.

Your ships should be technologically ahead enough that with a full aircraft complement you can take on navies that out-number you 2 to 1 with minimal casualties. That is where your job is, as you will be facing the Japanese head on. The Americans may, or may not be able to support you. Even if they cannot, you should be able to take on an AI Japan easily, and with coordination from Australia's starting navy you should be able to narrowly beat the Japanese player on water so long as they never hit land-fall.

Once you get done with the focus tree (around mid-late 1943), remember to begin to lower subject autonomy with the continuous focus, and in any other feasible means so as to be able to annex them by 1946, if the war is still on by then.
